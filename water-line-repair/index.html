What to do while you wait for a plumber

Four quick steps help whether the leak is on your side or the City's.

Close the house valve.

Turn it clockwise. If the meter stops moving, the leak is past that valve.

Read the meter.

Write down the reading and the time. Check it again after 30 minutes with no water in use.

Keep clear of the wet area.

Soft ground over a buried line can collapse, and standing water can hide electrical risks.

Do not dig.

Digging without marking lines can hit gas, power or fiber. Call Sunshine 811 first if digging is ever needed.

How a water line fails and how we find the leak

The service line is usually copper, polyethylene, PVC or older galvanized pipe. Each fails differently. Copper pits, polyethylene cracks at fittings, and galvanized steel rusts shut from the inside until it leaks at a joint.

We confirm a leak with the meter test, then pressure test the line. A line locator traces its path, and listening gear on the ground pinpoints the break. In some soils plastic pipe is quiet, so we use the locator and tracer gas where listening fails.

Low water pressure: four causes

Weak pressure is not always a leak. These are the usual reasons, in the order we check them.

A partly closed valve

The meter valve or house shutoff may have been bumped partly closed. A gate valve that is old can also fail to open fully. This is the quickest check.

A failing pressure regulator

Many homes have a pressure regulator near the main shutoff. A worn one can let pressure drop too low, or climb high enough to stress fittings. A gauge on a hose bib shows the reading. Most homes sit best between roughly 40 and 80 psi.

A leak or restriction in the line

A leak bleeds pressure before it reaches the house. Old galvanized pipe also narrows from rust and scale, and low pressure from this type of pipe is a sign to talk about repiping.

A problem on the utility side

If pressure drops across the street, hydrant flushing or a main repair may be the reason. Ask a neighbor and call the City.

Repair choices: dig or go trenchless

A spot repair means digging down to the leak, cutting out the bad section and joining new pipe with proper fittings. It is quick when the break is shallow and the yard is open. The ground is then backfilled and compacted.

Trenchless replacement pulls a new line through the old route from two small pits, which protects driveways, walls and mature trees. It needs a path that is clear enough to pull through, and it costs more to set up. We explain which suits your yard.
