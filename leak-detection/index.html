Emergency Leak Detection in Tavares, FL

.

Leak detection in Tavares, FL is the work of finding exactly where water escapes from a pipe, using instruments instead of demolition. If water is spreading, dripping through a ceiling or running somewhere you cannot see, call our 24/7 line and close the main shutoff valve while you wait.

Plenty of leaks never look like a flood. They show up as a meter that moves with every tap off, a bill that doubled, a brown ring on a ceiling or a wet strip in the lawn. A licensed plumber traces the source, explains the price before work starts, and books same-day appointments when available.

Signs of a leak that cannot wait

Some leaks are slow and some are not. These signs mean water is escaping right now, and each one is worth a call at any hour.

  • A meter dial or low-flow indicator moving while every fixture is off.
  • A water bill far above your usual months with no change in habits.
  • A ceiling stain that is growing, sagging or dripping.
  • A warm patch on the floor, or the sound of running water in a quiet house.
  • Soggy ground, a sunken strip or bright green grass in the yard with no rain.
  • A musty smell, peeling paint or baseboards that swell and bubble.

If water is actively coming through a ceiling, shut the main valve first and switch off breakers for that room if light fixtures are wet. Set a bucket under the drip and photograph the damage for your insurer before anything is moved. Our after-hours dispatcher can talk you through finding the shutoff.

Meter running or bill jumped: start with a home meter test

A water meter is the most honest leak detector in the house. It counts every gallon that passes, so if it moves while nothing is using water, that water is going somewhere you cannot see. A higher bill points the same way, although a stuck sprinkler timer, a running toilet or a houseful of guests can explain it too.

How to run the test yourself

Turn off every tap, the dishwasher, the washing machine and the ice maker, and pause the sprinkler timer. Open the meter box lid and look for the small leak indicator, often a triangle or star, or read the dial. If it turns, write down the full reading, wait 30 to 60 minutes without using water, and read it again. Any change means a leak.

Then check the toilets, because a silent flapper leak is the most common cause. Put a few drops of food coloring in each tank, wait 15 minutes without flushing, and look for color in the bowl.

What the result tells us

If the meter moves and the toilets are dry, close the main valve on the house side and watch the dial again. A meter that stops means the leak is past the valve, inside the house or in the yard lines it feeds. A meter that keeps moving points to the buried line between the meter and the house. Telling the dispatcher which one you saw saves real time on site.

Wet ceiling or stained wall: where is the water really from?

A ceiling stain has three common sources. A supply or drain pipe may be leaking above it, a roof or flashing may be failing, or the air conditioner may be sending condensate where it should not. In Florida the last one is very common, because the air handler often sits in the attic and its drain line clogs with algae.

We start with a moisture meter and a thermal camera to outline the wet area, then test the plumbing above it. That can mean running the upstairs shower and tub one at a time, pressure testing the supply lines, or dye testing a tub overflow gasket. We do not repair roofs or air conditioners, and if the water is not coming from a pipe we tell you so and point you to the right trade.

Under the house or in a crawlspace

Most homes around Tavares sit on a concrete slab, so there is no space underneath to crawl through. Older houses and some raised homes do have a crawlspace, and there a leak is often visible as dripping, stained joists or wet insulation. A spongy floor, a damp smell or a hissing sound under the boards are the usual clues.

If a crawlspace is wet, we check whether the water is plumbing or just groundwater after heavy rain. Closing the main valve and watching for new moisture is the quickest test. For a slab home, a leak under the floor is a different problem, and our slab leak repair page explains how those are located and fixed.

Buried leaks between the meter and the house

The water service line runs underground from the meter to the house, and it fails quietly. Signs include a meter that moves with the house valve shut, weak pressure at every tap, and a damp stripe or sinkhole-like dip in the yard. Soil can soak up a slow leak without it ever surfacing, so a dry yard does not rule it out.

We trace the pipe with a line locator, listen along its path, and mark the spot before anyone digs. Call Sunshine 811 before any excavation, because that line may share a trench with other utilities. Repairs on this section are covered under water line repair.

Sprinklers, pools and yard leaks

Outdoor leaks are easy to miss because the water disappears into the ground. Each type has its own signs.

Irrigation systems

A stuck valve, a cracked head or a broken lateral can waste water all night. Tavares provides reclaimed water for irrigation, so if your sprinklers run on reclaimed service, a leak there will not move your drinking water meter at all. Ask the City which supply your system uses before you read too much into the dial.

Pools and spas

A pool that drops faster than evaporation explains may have a leak in the fill line, the return piping or the equipment pad. We test and locate leaks in the plumbing that feeds the pool. We do not repair pool shells, liners or pool equipment, and a pool technician is the right call for those.

Yard and meter box

Check the meter box first. If it is full of water, the leak may be at the meter connection on the City side, or at the shutoff on yours. A running hose bib, a leaking backflow device and a cracked outdoor spigot are also common and simple to confirm.

How a leak-finding visit runs

  1. 01Talk and read the meter.

    We ask what you noticed, when it started and what the meter does with everything off. The reading you wrote down helps.

  2. 02Isolate with shutoffs and pressure.

    Closing valves in order splits the house into zones. A pressure gauge on each zone shows which one is losing water.

  3. 03Pinpoint with the right tool.

    Listening gear, thermal imaging, a line locator or tracer gas is chosen for the zone and pipe material, not used by habit.

  4. 04Confirm and explain options.

    Before any wall or floor is opened, we confirm the spot and explain the repair choices and the price. Same-day repair is possible when parts and schedule allow.

What affects the price of finding a leak

Leak detection is priced by the work involved, not a flat number, and we explain it before the work starts. Five things move the total.

How many methods are needed.

A meter test and one pressure test is quick. A leak that needs listening, imaging and a camera takes longer.

Size of the property.

Long pipe runs, outbuildings, pools and irrigation add zones to test.

Access to pipes.

Open garages and attics are easy. Finished walls, tile and slabs make listening harder.

Time of day.

Calls at night or on weekends can change the dispatch charge, and you hear about it first.

Whether repair follows.

Finding a leak and fixing it are different tasks, and the repair is priced once we see the pipe.

Some homeowners policies cover the cost of tearing out and rebuilding to reach a leak, and a few cover the detection itself when there is covered damage. Read your policy or ask your carrier before work, and keep your meter readings and photos.

Tavares local note: city line or your line?

The City of Tavares maintains more than 134 miles of water lines, and responsibility often changes at the meter but varies by property. Water leaking on the street side of the meter may be a City matter, and the City lists emergency water and sewer lines on its Water Division and Wastewater Division pages. If the leak is on the house side, it is ours to trace.

We cover Tavares and nearby towns, including Eustis and Mount Dora. If a leak turns out to be a failed pipe that is spraying, see burst pipe repair. If the leak is in a drain, the sewer inspection camera is the tool that finds it.
