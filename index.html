If water is moving right now

Four steps keep a plumbing problem small until a plumber arrives. None of them needs tools, and each takes only a minute or two.

Close the main valve.

Turn the main shutoff clockwise until it stops. It is usually near the front hose bib or in the garage. A valve inside the meter box may belong to the City, so wait for us if the house valve will not work.

Stop using the drains.

A backing-up drain means the line is blocked downstream. Run no water, flush nothing and keep drain chemicals out of it.

Cut power near water.

If the panel is dry and you can reach it without standing in water, switch off the breakers for wet rooms. Otherwise stay out and tell the dispatcher.

Take photos first.

Photograph the damage before anything is moved or dried. Your insurance carrier will ask for them.

These steps do not replace a plumber, but they cap the damage. When you call, tell the dispatcher what you already did.

What counts as a plumbing emergency

Some problems can wait for a morning visit and some cannot. If any line below describes your house, call now.

  • Water spraying or pouring from a pipe you cannot shut off.
  • Sewage backing up into a tub, shower or floor drain.
  • A water meter that keeps moving with every fixture turned off.
  • A toilet overflowing after the supply valve is closed.
  • A water heater tank leaking onto the floor.
  • A ceiling stain that is spreading or dripping.

Not on the list? Call anyway. A dispatcher can tell you in a minute whether a visit tonight makes sense, and a slow drip near a fitting that is already corroded can worsen into a burst pipe repair.

What makes plumbing different in Tavares

Tavares sits on a narrow isthmus between Lake Eustis and Lake Dora, about 118 feet above sea level. Lots near the water can stay wet for long stretches, which can hide a slow leak until the yard stays soggy. If your yard stays soggy in dry weather, a visit from our leak detection service is worth booking.

The City of Tavares pumps water from the Floridan Aquifer through six wells and maintains about 134 miles of water lines. Responsibility often changes at the meter, but it varies by property, so ask City of Tavares Utilities where yours ends. Our water line repair page explains how to tell which side a leak is on.

The city's published water quality report does not list a hardness figure, so scale on fixtures and water heaters is a guess until you test your tap. A home test kit or a call to City of Tavares Utilities settles it. If the result is high, our water softener and filtration page covers the options.

The city's wastewater system includes 70 lift stations that are monitored around the clock. A backup inside one house is usually a private line problem, and sewer line cleaning or sewer line repair handles that side. If the whole street is affected, call the city's wastewater line as well.
