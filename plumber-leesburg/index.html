Before We Arrive in Leesburg

A longer drive makes these first steps worth doing carefully.

Locate and test the main valve.

Turn it fully off and open a tap to confirm the flow stops. If it fails, tell the dispatcher right away.

Send a clear address.

Give the street, the cross street and the nearest big landmark. During large Leesburg events, such as Bikefest, which began in 1997 and draws very large crowds, roads near the lakefront can slow every vehicle.

Unplug and lift.

Pull appliance plugs on wet floors and move rugs, boxes and electronics. Do this only if the floor is not electrified.

Take photos and a short video.

Show the source, the flood line and any stained ceiling. A thirty-second clip is useful for insurance and for the plumber.

If the water is spreading toward neighbors in a duplex or condo, notify the property manager as soon as the shutoff is safe.

Leesburg water, 15 wells and a big pipe network

The City of Leesburg says its water comes from 15 Floridan aquifer wells and travels through 382 miles of mains to about 37,300 residential and 2,000 commercial customers. A system that large and old, established more than a century ago, has many street-side valves and meters of different ages. The city page lists no hardness figure, so a home test kit is the way to learn your water, not a guess.

A big network also means low pressure can come from several places. Check whether neighbors have the same problem, then check your own regulator and main valve. If only your house is affected, the cause is usually inside your lot.
