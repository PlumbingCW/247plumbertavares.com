Active leak or backup at a business: what to do first

A burst line, a toilet overflow, a backed-up floor drain or a failed water heater can close a business on the spot. The first call should say what you see, where the main shutoff is, and whether anyone is on site. A property manager can authorize work by phone if the manager has authority from the owner.

Before we arrive, shut the main, switch off power in wet areas and lift anything valuable. Photograph damage for the insurer, and keep receipts for any emergency purchases, since business policies often ask for them.

Leak detection in commercial buildings

A high water bill or a wet ceiling in a commercial building does not always mean a plumbing leak. Roof leaks, air conditioning condensate and sprinkler tests produce water that looks the same. We tell you honestly when the water source is not plumbing, and in those cases the right call is a roofer or an HVAC technician.

When the leak is plumbing, a pressure test isolates the line, acoustic listening finds the noise, and thermal imaging or moisture meters narrow it down. We cannot see through a slab or a sealed wall, so some searches end with a short list of likely spots and a plan to open the smallest area. For more on methods, see leak detection.

Backflow testing and cross-connection rules

Backflow assemblies protect the public water supply from contaminated water drawn backward into it. They sit on irrigation systems, fire lines, fountains and some kitchen and process equipment. The City of Tavares requires them to be tested annually, and the Water Division mails a test notice with a list of approved testers.

Use a tester from that list, and keep the paperwork. Call us about the repair or replacement work that a failed test can lead to, such as rebuilding a check valve or replacing a worn assembly. Confirm any tester's approval with the city before you hire.

Restaurants, grease traps and kitchen drains

A restaurant's drain line carries grease, starch and food solids that coat the pipe and stick to each other. A grease trap or interceptor captures fat before it reaches the sewer, but only if it is cleaned on schedule. Slow floor drains, odors and backups at the prep sink are early signs that it is overdue.

Pumping a large interceptor is usually done by a hauler with a tank truck, and we say so if that is the right service. We repair the trap, replace baffles and clear the lines downstream, and drain cleaning with cables or jets removes the buildup. City of Tavares Utilities runs the wastewater system, so ask the division about any grease rules that apply to your business.

Common problems in shops, offices and rentals

Business buildings fail in predictable ways, and catching them early keeps the doors open.

  • Slow or gurgling floor drains in a kitchen or restroom.
  • Toilets that overflow when another fixture drains.
  • A water heater that can no longer keep up with staff or customers.
  • Dripping flush valves on commercial toilets.
  • Low pressure on upper floors.
  • A water bill that has jumped with no change in use.
  • Wet carpet or ceiling tiles that return after drying.
  • A backflow test notice that has been ignored.

If several fixtures back up together, the cause is usually the building's main line, which is covered under sewer line cleaning.

What commercial plumbing costs

Commercial jobs are priced after we see the space, because buildings differ so much. These are the main factors.

Type and size of the building.

A small office and a restaurant with multiple kitchen lines are different jobs.

Access and working hours.

Work done after hours or around business hours may change scheduling and cost.

Equipment involved.

Commercial water heaters, flush valve toilets and backflow assemblies need parts that cost more than residential ones.

Extent of the problem.

A single stoppage is quick. A failing main line or repeated backups may call for a camera inspection first.

Permits and testing.

Some work needs inspections or documentation, which adds steps to the schedule.

Business property and interruption policies may cover sudden water damage and lost income. Many exclude gradual leaks and maintenance failures, so photograph everything and call your carrier early.

A phone call starts the process, and the plumber gives the price before touching a pipe.
