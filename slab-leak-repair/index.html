Emergency Slab Leak Repair in Tavares, FL

.

A slab leak in Tavares, FL is a break in a water line that runs under or inside the concrete foundation of a house. Our line answers 24/7, and if you suspect one, shut the main valve, stay off the wet floor and call while you wait.

Because most homes here are built on a slab, the pipes are hidden below the floor you walk on. That is why the first signs are often a warm patch, a hiss in a quiet room or a meter that never stops. What to do first when you suspect a slab leak

Four steps cost nothing and limit the damage while a plumber is on the way.

Close the main valve.

Turn the main shutoff clockwise at the front of the house or at the meter box. If the meter then stops, the leak is on the house side.

Write down the meter.

Record the reading before and after you close the valve. It tells the plumber how fast water is escaping.

Protect the floor.

Lift rugs and furniture off wet or warm areas, and switch off breakers for any room with standing water.

Photograph everything.

Take pictures of wet flooring, baseboards and the meter for your insurance claim before anything is dried.

Do not drill or chip the slab yourself. Post-tension cables or rebar can be under the surface, and a nick can turn a plumbing repair into a structural one.

How a slab leak shows itself

Hot water lines under the floor tend to give the earliest warning. A section of floor that is warm to bare feet when the heater is off often sits above a leaking hot line. A cold line leaks just as hard but reveals itself later, usually as damp carpet along a wall or a musty smell from under the tile.

Sound and numbers help as well. A faint hiss or rushing noise in a quiet house, weak hot water, and a bill that rises with no change in habits all fit a pressurized leak. Baseboards that swell, grout lines that darken and tile that lifts at the edges are signs the moisture has been there for a while.

Why slab lines fail around the lakes

Tavares sits on a narrow strip between Lake Eustis and Lake Dora at roughly 118 feet above sea level. Near the shoreline the water table can stand high for long stretches, so the soil around a slab stays damp and quietly attacks copper from the outside. Pinholes form where pipe touches concrete or sand that holds moisture.

Age and pressure add to it. Copper that has been rubbing against the slab through years of expansion and contraction wears through at bends. Homes with polybutylene supply lines from the late 1970s to the mid 1990s can fail at fittings, and a pressure regulator that has drifted high speeds up every one of these problems.

Spot repair, reroute or repipe: how we help you choose

The first leak is not the whole story. A pressure test after the repair shows whether the rest of the system holds, and if it does not, a single patch only buys time. We lay out each choice with the disruption it brings, so the decision is yours.

When a spot repair is enough

If one pinhole is the only failure and the line is otherwise sound, opening the slab at that point is the fastest fix. The plumber scans for rebar and cables, cuts a small opening, replaces the damaged piece and pressure tests before the concrete is patched. Flooring over the cut is replaced by a flooring contractor, not by us.

When rerouting makes more sense

A reroute abandons the leaking under-slab line and brings new PEX or copper through the attic to the fixtures. It avoids cutting the floor at every leak. The trade-off is access, since walls and ceilings need openings and patching, and it is usually done for a leak that has repeated or a line that is in poor shape.

When a repipe is worth discussing

If the pipe is the type that is known to fail, or leaks keep appearing, see our repiping service for how a whole-house replacement works. It costs more up front and often costs less than a string of separate repairs.

How the leak is located before anything is cut

  1. 01Confirm it is the slab.

    We rule out the toilets, fixtures and attic lines first, then close the main valve and watch the meter.

  2. 02Isolate the zone.

    A pressure test on hot and cold lines separately tells us which side is losing water, and which part of the house.

  3. 03Listen and image.

    Acoustic microphones on the floor and a thermal camera mark the spot, and tracer gas helps when the readings disagree.

  4. 04Mark and explain.

    We mark the floor, explain the options and price, and schedule the repair, often the same day when parts and crew are available.

What a slab leak repair costs

Prices vary because no two slabs are alike. These five things move the cost most.

Where the leak sits.

A leak near a wall or in a hallway is quicker to reach than one under a kitchen island or a tiled bath.

Floor finish.

Carpet lifts easily. Tile, stone and wood need careful removal and a flooring contractor afterward.

Repair method.

A spot repair, a reroute and a repipe are very different jobs in time and materials.

Number of leaks.

A second or third leak on the same line often shifts the best option toward replacing the line.

Water damage.

Drying and rebuilding is separate work, and slab homes can hide moisture under flooring for a long time.

Many policies cover resulting water damage and some cover the cost of reaching the leak, but they often exclude the worn pipe itself. Call your carrier before repairs, and keep the plumber's report and your photos.

Worth knowing about mold and your water bill

Moisture under flooring can feed mold within days in Florida's humid air. If a carpet smells musty or a baseboard is soft, ask for a moisture reading as part of the visit. A leak that has run for weeks may also have pushed your water bill far above normal, so keep your meter readings.
