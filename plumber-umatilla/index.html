Rotten-egg smell: the Umatilla question

Many Umatilla callers ask about a sulfur smell. Hydrogen sulfide is what gives off a rotten-egg odor, and the City of Umatilla's report says its first water plant aerates the water to reduce it before chlorine is added. So a faint smell can be a property of the source water, while a strong smell needs a closer look.

The test is simple. Run only the cold tap for a minute, then only the hot. If the smell shows up in the hot water alone, the water heater is the likely cause, since an anode rod can react with sulfur compounds in the tank.

Smell at every tap

If cold and hot both smell, check a few other things. Look at the drain traps for dry or slow fixtures, because a dry trap lets sewer gas into the room. If the smell persists, call the City of Umatilla utility and then call us if you suspect a plumbing cause.

Smell in hot water only

A tank that smells only when you draw hot water often needs flushing and an anode rod check. In some cases a replacement rod fixes it, and in others the tank is too old to be worth the repair.

Before We Arrive in Umatilla

A longer drive makes the steps you take before we arrive more important.

Check the water source.

Know whether your house uses the city system or a private well. That tells us whether a pressure tank, pump or utility valve is part of the problem.

Shut the right valve.

For a leak at a fixture, use the small valve under it. For a spreading leak, close the main near the meter or the pressure tank.

Stop drain use.

If water backs up, hold off on laundry and showers. Septic homes in particular suffer when extra water goes into a full system.

Leave the way clear.

Open the gate, secure pets and clear the path to the water heater or meter so we can begin at once.

If the problem is a main break in the street, the City of Umatilla Utilities handles it. For anything past your meter, call us.
