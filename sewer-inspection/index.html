Sewer Inspection in Tavares, FL

.

A sewer inspection in Tavares, FL means sending a small waterproof camera on a flexible cable through your sewer line to see its condition from the inside. It is the planned way to learn why drains keep clogging, whether a line is safe to buy, and where a repair should start. Same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows.

A licensed plumber runs the camera, watches the monitor with you and explains what each image means. The price is stated before work starts, and any repair that follows is discussed as a separate decision.

What the camera can show you

The camera head carries a light and a lens, and it moves from the cleanout toward the street or the tank. On the monitor we see the pipe wall, the joints and anything inside. A trained eye reads the images for cracks, offset joints, roots, scale, grease and low spots.

A locator at ground level picks up the signal from the camera head, which lets us mark the depth and path of the line. That information is useful for a repair plan, a landscaping project or a new addition that might sit over the pipe.

Where a camera stops: honest limits

A camera cannot see through a line that is full of water or waste, and it cannot judge the soil outside the pipe. Heavy buildup may need clearing before it can pass, and in that case we tell you the cleaning is a separate step. A camera also does not measure flow, so a pipe that looks sound can still have a slow section.

It also cannot see inside a collapsed section. If the camera stops where the pipe has fallen in, that stopping point tells us where to dig, and the rest of the line stays unseen until the repair is done.

Good reasons to book an inspection

You do not need a backup to benefit from a look inside the line. These are the most common reasons.

  • You are buying or selling a house and want to know the condition of the line.
  • Drains clog again within weeks of being cleared.
  • You hear gurgling when the washer or a toilet drains.
  • A damp spot or sewer smell shows up in the yard.
  • Large trees stand near the line, or roots were found before.
  • A previous repair needs a check, or a contractor needs to see the line first.

Buying or selling: what an inspection tells you

A general home inspection looks at the house, not the buried pipe. A sewer camera inspection fills that gap, and it is a common step before closing on a home with mature trees or older plumbing. For a seller, a clear result is something to show a buyer. For a buyer, a finding gives you facts to bring to the negotiation.

Schedule it early, since a repair estimate takes time to arrange. Ask what you will receive after the visit, such as footage or a written summary, so you can share it with your agent or inspector.

How a camera inspection goes

  1. 01Find the access point.

    We use the cleanout, a pulled toilet or a vent if there is no cleanout. A missing cleanout is itself a finding worth fixing.

  2. 02Clear what blocks the view.

    If grease or debris blocks the lens, a cable or jet clears a path first, and we explain that before charging for it.

  3. 03Run the camera and locate.

    The camera moves toward the street or tank while the locator marks the path and depth at problem spots.

  4. 04Review it with you.

    We go over what was seen, which items need action, and which are only worth watching.

When to clean the line before the camera

If the line is heavily clogged, the picture will be poor and may miss the damage that caused the clog. In that case hydro-jetting scours grease and roots from the pipe wall, and the camera then follows to show the pipe underneath. A line that has simply been clogged and cleared once may not need a camera, while a line that clogs repeatedly does.

If sewage is already backing up into the house, that is a clearing job first. Our sewer line cleaning page covers what to do.

What affects the price of a sewer inspection

An inspection is priced by the work involved, and the number is explained before the camera goes in. Four things matter most.

Access to the line.

A cleanout makes the job straightforward. Pulling a toilet to get in takes longer.

Length and condition of the run.

A short, clear line is quick. A long run with buildup takes more time.

Cleaning needed first.

Clearing grease or roots before the camera is extra work and is quoted separately.

Locating and marking.

Marking the path and depth for a repair or a project adds time.

Home insurance rarely pays for an inspection by itself. If a covered loss such as a sudden backup is involved, the carrier may want the camera report, so keep it with your claim papers.

A phone call starts the process, and the plumber gives the price before touching a pipe.
