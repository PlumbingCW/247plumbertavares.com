Hot Water Slab Leak Repair in Tavares, FL

.

A hot water slab leak is a break in the hot supply line that runs under the concrete floor of a house, and in Tavares, FL most homes have slab floors. The leak often shows as a warm spot on the floor, a water heater that never stops working or a bill that rises with no change in habits.

This guide explains the signs, how plumbers locate the leak without tearing up the house, the repair routes and what you can safely check yourself.

By the Tavares 24/7 Plumbing team. Published October 15, 2026.

How a hot line differs from a cold one

A hot water line leaks into the soil under the slab and warms the concrete above it. Copper in this position is attacked from the outside by soil and moisture and from the inside by heat, which is why hot lines often fail before cold ones.

A cold line leak raises bills and softens flooring but rarely warms the floor. A warm spot is the clearest clue that a hot line is the source.

Clues to look for

One sign alone may have another cause, but two or three together make a slab leak likely.

  • A floor area that feels warm barefoot.
  • A water heater that runs all the time or never gets fully hot.
  • A sudden jump in the water bill.
  • The sound of running water when all fixtures are off.
  • Low hot water pressure at the farthest faucets.
  • Damp carpet, lifting tile or a musty smell near one spot.

A sound like running water at night is easy to hear in a quiet house. Listen with your ear near the floor before bed.

Confirm it before you call

Turn off every fixture and appliance that uses water. Look at the meter dial or the small triangle on it. If it moves, water is going somewhere. Then shut the water heater inlet valve and recheck. If the meter slows or stops, the leak is on the hot side.

Do the same with the main valve closed. If a spinning meter does not change when you close the heater inlet, the cold side is leaking instead.

How the leak gets located

Acoustic listening picks up the hiss of escaping water through the concrete. A pressure test isolates a line and shows whether it holds. Thermal imaging can map the warm path of hot water under a floor. Together these tools narrow the leak to a patch of slab a few feet across.

No single tool is perfect. Concrete thickness, pipe depth and background noise all affect results, and we say so when readings are uncertain.

Safety while you wait

Hot water under pressure can scald, so do not touch a leak coming through a crack. Shut off the water heater and, if the heater is electric, its breaker. Keep electrical items and cords off a wet floor and photograph any damage for your insurer.

Questions to ask before you approve a repair

Ask what tools were used to locate the leak and how sure the plumber is of the spot. Ask whether the line is copper, CPVC or polybutylene, how old it is and whether other lines nearby show wear. Ask how the floor will be patched and how long the concrete must cure.

A plumber should explain the price before work starts and tell you what is not included, such as flooring, paint and cabinets. Verify the contractor at MyFloridaLicense.com by typing the name into the Verify a License tool.

Why waiting makes it worse

Water under a slab can wash away soil, leaving a void that lets the floor settle. It also feeds mold in wall cavities and can damage flooring and baseboards that are far from the break. Hot water adds a constant utility cost, since the heater keeps heating water that leaks away. Even a small hot leak can add up on the monthly bill.

A small leak that is easy to reroute today can become a floor-wide repair if it continues for months. Early action keeps the repair small and the disruption short.

Tavares local note: the meter and the age of the house

The City of Tavares Utilities maintains about 134 miles of water lines, and your meter is the dividing line between city pipe and yours. A meter that runs with everything off, as described above, is a leak inside your property line.

Homes built from the late 1970s into the mid 1990s in this area may have polybutylene supply piping, which fails differently from copper and often points toward repiping. Ask about the pipe type before choosing a spot repair, and ask the City of Tavares Building Division about permit rules.
