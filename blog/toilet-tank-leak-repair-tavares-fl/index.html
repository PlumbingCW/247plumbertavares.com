Toilet Tank Leak Repair in Tavares, FL

.

Toilet tank leak repair in Tavares, FL starts with one question: is the water leaking out of the tank onto the floor, or leaking inside from the tank into the bowl? The two problems look alike but have different fixes.

A quiet inside leak can waste a lot of water over a month. A floor leak can rot the subfloor. Here is how to tell which one you have, and what to fix.

By the Tavares 24/7 Plumbing team. Published October 15, 2026.

Step one: the dye test

Take the tank lid off and set it somewhere safe, because porcelain lids crack easily. Drop a few drops of food coloring or a dye tablet into the tank water. Do not flush.

Wait fifteen to twenty minutes, and keep everyone from flushing in that time. If color shows up in the bowl, the leak is inside, from the flapper or fill valve. If the bowl stays clear but you see drips on the floor, the leak is outside the tank, from the supply line, the bolts or the wax ring.

Signs that point to each cause

Match what you see and hear to the likely part.

  • Hissing or running water after a flush points to the fill valve.
  • Color in the bowl after a dye test points to the flapper.
  • Drips at the base of the tank point to tank bolts or washers.
  • Wet floor at the wall point to the supply line or shutoff valve.
  • Rocking toilet with seepage at the base points to the wax ring.
  • Water pooling only after a flush suggests a cracked tank or bowl.

A cracked porcelain tank cannot be repaired reliably, and a hairline crack that weeps only when the tank is full is easy to miss. Replace the tank or the whole toilet. If the floor around the toilet stays damp after you fix the parts above, the water may be coming from a pipe in the slab rather than the fixture, and a leak detection visit can sort that out.

Fix the flapper or the fill valve

  1. 01Shut off and drain.

    Turn the oval-handled valve behind the toilet clockwise, flush, and sponge out the remaining water.

  2. 02Swap the flapper.

    Unhook it from the overflow pegs, match the new one by size, and set the chain with a little slack.

  3. 03Replace the fill valve.

    Disconnect the supply nut under the tank, loosen the locknut, lift the old valve out, and install a new one at the same height.

  4. 04Refill and retest.

    Open the valve slowly, adjust the water level a half inch below the overflow tube, and repeat the dye test.

Tank bolts and the seal between tank and bowl

If the drip appears where the tank meets the bowl, the tank bolts or the rubber washers are failing. Tighten the nuts a little at a time, alternating sides, and stop as soon as the leak stops. Porcelain cracks if you overtighten.

If tightening does not help, the washers need replacing. You will drain the tank, remove the bolts, lift the tank off, and replace the gasket and washers. The tank is heavy and slippery, so work with a helper and set it on a towel. Replace the bolts and washers as a set, and use the rubber washer on the inside of the tank, not the outside.

Tavares local note: chlorinated water and rubber parts

The City of Tavares disinfects its groundwater with chlorine, averaging 0.6 ppm in the 2023 report. That level is safe to drink, but chlorine slowly breaks down rubber and soft plastic. Flappers and fill valve seals are often the first parts to go, so replace them when they feel soft, sticky or warped.

Tavares Utilities also publishes no hardness figure. If you see crusty mineral deposits on a flapper seat or fill valve, the water may be leaving scale there. Wipe the seat with a cloth and a little vinegar before you install a new part. For problems beyond the tank, our toilet repair service covers the whole fixture.

When to stop and call a plumber

Call if the supply line or shutoff valve leaks and will not stop with a gentle snug. Call if the toilet rocks, if the floor feels soft near the base, or if you see water coming through a ceiling below. Those signs point to a failed wax ring or a leak that has already spread.

Also call if you have replaced the flapper and fill valve and the dye still reaches the bowl. The flush valve seat may be worn or pitted, and fixing it can mean replacing the whole toilet. A licensed plumber in Eustis or Mount Dora can check it and quote the work, and the same goes in Tavares.

Tools and parts to have ready

You need an adjustable wrench, a bucket, a sponge, a towel, a flashlight, food coloring and a screwdriver. Take a photo of the old part before you go to the store, and write down the brand name stamped inside the tank. Fill valves and flappers come in several sizes, and many shops sell a kit that bundles the flapper, fill valve and bolts, and matching them by photo saves a second trip.

Buy a universal flapper only if the tank is a standard type. Tanks with a wide three-inch flush opening need a larger flapper than the usual two-inch type, and picking the wrong one is a common reason a repair fails. Some low-flow toilets need a specific model, and a mismatched flapper can leak or cut the flush short.
