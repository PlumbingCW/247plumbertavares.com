Water Pipe Heat Tape in Tavares, FL: When It Matters

.

Heat tape is an electric cable that wraps a water pipe and keeps it from freezing, and most Tavares, FL homes never need it. A hard freeze is rare here, so the question is not whether to cover every pipe but which few pipes are exposed enough to be at risk.

This guide explains where heat tape actually matters, how to install it safely, what to use instead and what to do if a pipe has already frozen.

By the Tavares 24/7 Plumbing team. Published October 15, 2026.

Do you need heat tape in Florida at all

Usually not. A typical Tavares home keeps its supply pipes inside the slab, in interior walls or in the attic, where temperatures usually stay above freezing, though attic lines can still be at risk in a long hard freeze. The pipes at risk are the ones outdoors or in unheated spaces.

A pipe freezes when the air around it stays below 32 degrees for several hours and the water inside is not moving. A brief dip to freezing at dawn rarely does damage, but a long hard freeze does. Plan for the exposed pipes, not the whole house.

Which pipes are worth protecting

How heat tape works and the two kinds

Heat tape runs on household current and warms the pipe it touches. A constant-wattage cable produces the same heat along its length. A self-regulating cable gives more heat where the pipe is colder and less where it is already warm, which makes it safer and more efficient.

Plug-in kits for exposed spigots and short runs are common at hardware stores. Match the kit to your pipe material, since some plastic pipe has temperature limits, and read the label before you buy.

Installing heat tape without creating a hazard

  1. 01Choose the right product.

    Pick a cable rated for the pipe length and material. Self-regulating is the better choice, and the package should show an approval mark from a recognized testing lab.

  2. 02Wrap the pipe as directed.

    Run it straight along the pipe or spiral it as the maker says. Never overlap a constant-wattage cable on itself, because overlapping can overheat and start a fire.

  3. 03Cover with pipe insulation.

    Wrap foam or fiberglass insulation over the tape so heat stays at the pipe. Keep insulation off the plug and the thermostat end.

  4. 04Plug into a protected outlet.

    Use a GFCI outlet, check the cable each year for cracks and unplug it when the cold season ends. Do not use extension cords that are not rated for the load.

When to avoid heat tape

Skip it on pipes inside insulated walls, near stored paper or paint, or on a cable that looks scorched or frayed. Replace damaged tape instead of repairing it, and never cut a plug-in cable to length unless the maker says you can.

If a pipe freezes or splits anyway

Shut off the main valve, open the faucet the pipe feeds, and warm the pipe slowly with a hair dryer on low or warm towels. Do not use an open flame. Keep the faucet open so pressure can bleed off as the ice melts.

If the pipe has split, you may see water only after it thaws, so check the area when temperatures rise. A cracked line needs a repair, and a pipe that froze once is more likely to freeze again, which is worth fixing at the source.

A few habits that keep exposed pipes safe

Before a forecast freeze, disconnect garden hoses, since a hose left attached traps water in the spigot. Cover spigots with foam domes, and open the cabinet doors under sinks on outside walls so house warmth reaches the pipes. Know where your main shutoff is before you need it.

If you leave town during cold weather, keep the heat on at a moderate setting and ask someone to check the house. A split pipe in an empty house can run for days.

Tavares local note: freezes are rare but real

Central Florida does get occasional hard freezes. The Great Freeze of 1894 to 1895 and another in 1898 to 1899 hit nearby Eustis and devastated the citrus economy. That history is a good reminder that rare does not mean never, so protect the few pipes that sit outside.

The City of Tavares Water Division requires backflow preventers to be tested every year, and many of those assemblies sit above ground beside a house or yard. Those are the pipes to cover on a freeze night. Ask the Water Division about your assembly before you handle it.

Where to get help

If an exposed line keeps freezing, we can reroute or insulate it with proper water line repair work, or reset a split section after a freeze. For a wider upgrade of old, vulnerable pipe, repiping is the permanent fix.

The same advice applies if you live in Umatilla or Leesburg, since a cold night reaches all of Lake County and only the exposed pipes are at risk.
