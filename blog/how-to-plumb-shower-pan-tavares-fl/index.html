How to Plumb a Shower Pan in Tavares, FL

.

Plumbing a shower pan means connecting the pan's drain body to a trap and a drain line, then proving the connection holds water before any tile or finish goes on. Homeowners in Tavares, FL ask about it most when a bathroom remodel is half planned and the tub is already out.

This guide walks through the order of work, the parts involved, which pieces a careful DIYer can handle and where a licensed plumber and the building department come in.

By the Tavares 24/7 Plumbing team. Published October 15, 2026.

The order of work, from rough-in to water test

  1. 01Confirm the drain location.

    Find the existing tub or shower drain and measure from two walls. A pan with a center drain needs the line within an inch or so of its final spot, so check before you buy the pan.

  2. 02Rough in the trap and line.

    A shower needs its own trap, usually two inches, set below the pan and vented so it cannot siphon dry. The line slopes toward the main drain at a steady fall, and it must be supported, not left to sag.

  3. 03Set the pan and connect the drain body.

    A prefab pan sits on a mortar or foam bed so it cannot flex. The drain body is clamped or glued to the pan, then joined to the trap with the fitting the manufacturer specifies.

  4. 04Test before you cover anything.

    Plug the drain, fill the pan to just under the curb and mark the water line. If the mark has not dropped after a few hours, the seal and the drain joint are tight.

What a pan and its drain are made of

A shower pan is the waterproof floor of the stall. It can be a one-piece fiberglass or acrylic base, a molded solid-surface base, or a field-built pan made from a sloped mortar bed and a waterproof liner or membrane. The drain body is the part that ties that floor to your plumbing.

Waste pipe in most homes here is white PVC or black ABS. Cement for PVC and for ABS are different products, and joining the two materials needs a transition fitting made for it. Mixing the wrong glue is one of the quieter mistakes that leads to a slow weep under a finished floor.

Prefab base or built-up pan

A prefab base is faster and has fewer places to fail, since the waterproof surface arrives already formed. A built-up pan allows custom size and tile, but the membrane, the clamping drain ring and the slope all have to be right the first time.

Why the trap and vent matter

The trap holds a plug of water that blocks sewer gas. Without a proper vent, a draining shower can pull that water out of the trap, and the bathroom smells like a sewer. Vent layout is the part most often misjudged on a remodel.

Safety limits and mistakes that cost the most

Shut off the water to the bathroom before demolition and cap the open supply stubs. Wear eye protection when you chip tile and use a respirator when cutting cement board or concrete, since the dust is hazardous to breathe.

The most expensive errors are a flat or backward slope on the drain line, a pan that flexes underfoot and a pinched liner at the drain ring. A leak under a pan rarely shows for weeks, then appears as a stain on the room next door.

When to stop and call

Stop if you find cast-iron drain pipe, if you see water seeping up from the slab, or if the trap arm will not reach the main line at a workable slope. Those cases turn a bathroom upgrade into a drain repair, and a plumber should look before you build anything over it.

Questions to settle before you buy or begin

Write down the size of the room, the drain location and the pan model before you shop. Check the pan maker's installation sheet, since each maker names the drain body, the allowed bed thickness and the curb height. Most returns happen because a drain body does not match the pan.

Decide who is doing what before the first cut. Splitting the work is common: a homeowner handles demolition, a plumber handles the trap and vent, and a tile setter handles the finish. A clear handoff avoids a pan that is set before the plumbing is approved.

Tavares local note: slab floors and permits

Most Tavares homes are built on a concrete slab, so moving a shower drain usually means breaking out concrete and rebuilding the line below. If you are only swapping a pan over the existing drain, the slab stays untouched, which is the cheapest path. Plan the new pan around the old drain location whenever you can.

Permit rules vary, so ask the City of Tavares Building Division at 201 E. Main Street before you cut anything. They can tell you whether a pan swap, a drain move or a full bath remodel needs a permit and inspection. A homeowner who skips that call can be asked to open finished walls later.

Getting the drain right before the finish goes on

A shower drain is part of the home's waste system, so it follows the Florida Building Code, Plumbing. We do not state specific code numbers here because they change, and your inspector or plumber can confirm what applies to your layout.

If you are moving the drain, adding a bath or opening a wall that holds supply lines, our plumbing installation team can rough in the drain, trap and vent and leave it ready for your pan. If the old drain is slow, check it first with drain cleaning so you do not tile over a partial clog.
