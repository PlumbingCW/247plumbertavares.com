Water Leak Detection Sensors for Tavares, FL Homes

.

A water leak detection sensor is a small battery device that sounds an alarm or sends a phone alert when it touches water. In a Tavares, FL home, where most houses sit on a slab, an early alert can mean the difference between a wet mat and a ruined floor.

This guide covers the sensor types, the places that matter most, and the honest limit: a sensor tells you water is out, but not where a hidden pressurized line is failing.

By the Tavares 24/7 Plumbing team. Published October 15, 2026.

What a leak sensor does and does not do

A basic sensor has two metal probes on its base. When water bridges them, a circuit closes and a siren sounds. Smart versions add Wi-Fi and push an alert to your phone, which matters when you are away.

A sensor only detects water at its own spot. It cannot see inside a wall or under a slab. If a pressurized line is leaking in a place with no sensor, the first sign may be a high bill, not an alarm.

Sensor types compared

Pick by what you want to protect, not by the longest feature list. Batteries also matter, because a dead sensor is worse than none when you trust it.

Seven places to put a sensor first

Start where leaks most often begin, then expand. Put one on the floor, flat, within a few inches of the likely drip point.

  • Next to the water heater, or inside its drain pan.
  • Under the kitchen sink near the supply lines.
  • Behind the dishwasher, if you can reach the floor there.
  • Beside the washing machine hoses.
  • Behind each toilet, near the supply line.
  • Under the refrigerator ice maker line.
  • Near the air handler drain pan, where condensate can overflow.

Test each sensor monthly by touching the probes with a damp finger. Replace batteries on the schedule printed on the box. Dust the sensor when you clean the floor, since a film of grime can slow its response.

Tavares local note: lakes, rain and slab floors

Tavares sits on an isthmus between Lake Eustis and Lake Dora, and lots near the lakes can have a high water table. After heavy rain, ground moisture and overloaded drains are real possibilities. A sensor near the garage door and by the air handler can warn you whether water is coming from outside or from a pipe.

Because most Tavares houses are on slab, a pressurized leak under the floor cannot be seen. Check your water meter. Shut every fixture off, then watch the leak indicator or the dial. If it keeps moving, you have a leak somewhere past the meter. The City of Tavares maintains more than 134 miles of water lines, and it lists emergency numbers on its Water Division page if the problem is on the street side.

Installing a sensor yourself

Most sensors need no tools. Peel the backing, press the sensor to a clean, dry floor, and pair it with your phone if it is a smart model. Wipe the floor first, because dust can stop the adhesive from holding.

Placement matters more than price. A sensor set on a shelf or a counter will never get wet, so lay it flat on the floor at the low point where water would pool.

Smart shutoff valves are different. They cut into a water line, need correct sizing, and should be set in a spot that is easy to reach. If you are not comfortable with fittings, use a licensed plumber so a bad joint does not create the leak you were trying to prevent.

When to call a plumber after an alert

If a sensor goes off and the puddle is from an obvious spill, dry it and reset. If the area stays damp, if the sensor trips again within a day, or if you see a stain growing on a wall, treat it as a leak. Close the nearest shutoff valve and call. Do not wait to see if it dries, because a seep behind a wall keeps feeding mold.

For leaks you cannot see, a plumber uses acoustic listening gear, pressure testing and thermal imaging to find the spot before opening any floor. Insurance and records

Many insurers look more kindly on homes with automatic shutoff devices, though discounts vary, so ask your agent. After any leak, photograph the damage before drying and keep your sensor alert as a time stamp.

Write down the date you noticed the water, the spot, and what you shut off. A short, dated record, plus a few phone photos of the wet spot and the sensor, makes a claim conversation much easier and helps a plumber trace where the water started.
