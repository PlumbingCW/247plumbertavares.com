What Is a Sewer Line Inspection in Tavares, FL?

.

A sewer line inspection is a camera survey of the pipe that carries waste from your house to the street or septic tank. Homeowners in Tavares, FL book one before a purchase, after repeat clogs or when a drain backs up. The camera shows the real condition of the pipe instead of guesswork.

This guide covers how the inspection works, what it can and cannot find, how to read the results and when it is worth the visit.

By the Tavares 24/7 Plumbing team. Published October 15, 2026.

How the camera inspection works

A technician feeds a waterproof camera on a flexible rod into the line through a cleanout. The camera sends live video to a screen. A small transmitter in the head lets a locator find its position and depth at the surface.

The crew records the run, notes distances from the entry point and marks problem spots. You can ask to watch the screen and keep a copy of the footage.

What the camera can reveal

Most findings fall into the categories below, and each leads to a different repair.

What the camera cannot do

It cannot see through a line packed with waste, so a clean-out pass often comes first. It cannot always tell a tiny leak from condensation, and a pipe that leaks only under pressure may need a separate test. It also cannot measure ground conditions around the pipe.

We tell you where the video is incomplete and what additional test would settle it, so you are not asked to guess.

What happens on the day

  1. 01Locate the cleanout.

    The crew finds the cleanout or removes a toilet if there is none. Clear the area and tell them about past clogs.

  2. 02Clear the line if needed.

    A blocked pipe is cleaned enough for the camera to pass through and see the walls.

  3. 03Run and record the camera.

    The camera travels toward the main, and the locator marks defects on the ground.

  4. 04Review the findings.

    You see the footage, hear what each defect means and get options in plain terms.

When an inspection is worth booking

Book one before buying a home, especially an older house or one with mature trees. Book one after a second clog in the same line, when odors appear with no source, or when a drain backs up after a rain. It also makes sense before a major yard project that might damage the pipe.

A pre-purchase inspection can protect you from inheriting a failing line. Ask the seller for permission and give the plumber access to the cleanout.

Reading the results like a homeowner

The footage is easier to understand if you know three terms. A joint is where two pipes meet and is the most common failure point. A belly is a low spot that holds water. An offset is where one pipe has shifted from the next and leaves a lip that catches waste.

Ask for the distance of each defect from the cleanout. A defect near the house is usually cheaper to reach than one near the street or deep under a driveway. Ask which defects need action now and which can be watched. A good plumber will rank them for you in plain words.

Inspection compared with a cleaning visit

A cleaning clears the pipe, and an inspection shows why it clogged. If the line has been cleaned twice in a year, an inspection is usually the better next step, since the cleaning only repeats the symptom. The two can happen in one visit when the line is blocked.

A camera is not a substitute for a pressure or leak test when the question is a hidden leak. Tell the plumber what you are trying to learn, and they can bring the right tool. If you only want to know the pipe condition before a purchase, say so when you book.

Seller, buyer and landlord situations

Buyers can add a sewer inspection to their review period. Sellers can book one before listing to remove a source of surprise. Landlords can use the footage as a maintenance record.

Ask the crew to note any spot they were unsure about, so a later visit can check it again. Keep the footage with your closing papers or home file. It also makes a second opinion easy to get. It gives the next plumber a map of the line, and it shows how the pipe changed from one visit to the next.

Tavares local note: the city side and your side

The City of Tavares treats wastewater at its Woodlea Road water reclamation facility and monitors 70 lift stations. Ask the utility where its responsibility ends and yours begins, since a camera run can show how far your own line extends before it meets the main.

If you are buying near the lakes, a high water table can push groundwater into a cracked pipe. Ask the City of Tavares Building Division at 201 E. Main Street how permits apply if the camera finds a repair is needed.
