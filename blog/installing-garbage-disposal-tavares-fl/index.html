Installing a Garbage Disposal in Tavares, FL

.

Installing a garbage disposal in Tavares, FL means mounting a motor unit under the sink flange, connecting its drain to the trap, and wiring or plugging it in. A handy homeowner can swap a like-for-like unit in a couple of hours.

A first-time install, a hardwired circuit or a sink with no existing power is a different job. This guide explains what is involved so you can decide who does the work.

By the Tavares 24/7 Plumbing team. Published October 15, 2026.

Should you replace it or repair it?

A disposal that hums but does not spin is often jammed, and the hex-key socket on the underside usually frees it. A unit that is silent may only need its red reset button pressed. A leaking housing or a rusted body means replacement.

Check these first before you spend money. Our garbage disposal repair service covers those quick fixes, and a failed unit older than its warranty is a fair candidate for a new one. Also ask whether the sink drains slowly, since a clogged trap can look like a bad disposal.

Which horsepower to buy

Bigger motors grind finer and jam less, but they are heavier and need more clearance under the sink. A heavier unit also puts more load on the sink flange, so check that the mounting ring seats flat on a firm sink.

The installation sequence

  1. 01Cut power and clear the cabinet.

    Switch off the breaker, confirm with a voltage tester, and empty the cabinet under the sink.

  2. 02Remove the old unit.

    Disconnect the drain pipe and dishwasher hose, twist the unit off the mounting ring, and unwire it if hardwired.

  3. 03Mount the new flange.

    Set plumber's putty under the sink flange, seat the new mounting ring, and tighten it evenly from below.

  4. 04Connect, wire and test.

    Hang the disposal, connect the drain and dishwasher hose, wire or plug in the cord, then run water and check for leaks.

The dishwasher knockout plug

If the disposal will connect to a dishwasher, there is a small plastic plug inside the dishwasher inlet that must be knocked out. Many people forget, and the dishwasher then refuses to drain. Tap it out with a screwdriver and hammer, and fish the plug out of the disposal chamber.

If you are not connecting a dishwasher, leave the plug in place. Otherwise water will pour out of the open port.

Electrical: plug or hardwire

Some kitchens have a dedicated outlet under the sink, and most disposals sold today can use a plug-in cord kit. A cord must reach the outlet without stretching or crossing the drain pipes. Others are hardwired to a switch. A switch and a grounded circuit must already be in place.

If there is no power at the sink, the project becomes an electrical job. Adding a circuit, a switch or a GFCI outlet belongs to a licensed professional. Do not splice into a lighting circuit to save time.

When to hand the job to a plumber

Call a plumber if the drain trap needs to move, if the cabinet space is too tight, or if the sink flange is a different size than the new mounting ring. Also call if your old drain pipes are corroded or if you smell sewer gas.

A plumber can handle a first-time install, new drain lines and any leaks you find on the first test. We take on installs through plumbing installation as well, and a licensed plumber in Leesburg or Eustis can take the same job.

Planning the drain connection

A disposal drains through a discharge elbow into the sink trap, and that elbow sits at a set height. If the new unit's outlet is lower or higher than the old one, the trap arm may need to be cut, extended or reangled. Dry fit the pieces before you glue or tighten anything.

Slope matters too. The trap arm should pitch gently toward the wall drain so water does not sit in it and smell. Use slip joint washers that are in good shape, and replace any brittle ones. Tighten each nut by hand and then a half turn with pliers.

Safety rules that matter

Never put your hand in a disposal, even when it is off. Use tongs or pliers to remove an object. Always switch off power at the breaker before reaching for a wrench near the unit. Check with a tester, because wall switches can fail closed. Wear eye protection when you work under the sink, since old rust and debris fall when the unit comes loose.
