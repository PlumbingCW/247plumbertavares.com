Emergency Faucet Repair in Tavares, FL

.

Faucet repair in Tavares, FL covers sink, tub and shower faucets, the shutoff valves under them, and the outdoor hose bibs that ride on the same supply. Our dispatch line is open 24/7 for the day a valve breaks off and water will not stop. Look under the sink first: the small oval or round stops on the two supply lines each turn clockwise to close.

If those stops spin freely or leak when you touch them, close the main water valve for the house and call. A licensed plumber can replace a failed stop or cartridge, explain the price before work starts, and offer a same-day appointment when the schedule allows.

A valve that will not shut off right now

The worst faucet call is the one where the shutoff itself fails. The stem snaps, the handle spins with nothing behind it, or water sprays from the packing nut when you try to close it. In that case the only control left is the main water valve, usually near the front hose bib, in the garage, or at the meter box.

Once the main is off, open the lowest faucet in the house to drain the line and take photos of the wet area. Do not keep forcing a stuck stop, because a cracked valve body sends water straight into the cabinet. Call and tell the dispatcher what the valve did, so the plumber arrives with the right stop, supply lines and a replacement cartridge.

Faucet problems worth a call

A faucet that drips once a second wastes water around the clock, and the part that causes it keeps wearing. These problems should not be left.

  • A steady drip from the spout with the handle fully closed.
  • Water around the base of the faucet or under the handle.
  • Hot and cold swapped, or a handle that spins without effect.
  • Low flow from one faucet only, which suggests a clogged aerator or cartridge.
  • A tub spout that keeps running when the shower diverter is pulled.
  • Shower temperature that jumps between hot and cold.
  • A wet cabinet floor or a musty smell under the sink.
  • A supply stop that weeps at the nut or does not turn.

Low flow from one faucet is nearly always local, usually a clogged aerator or worn cartridge. If every faucet is weak, look at the line pressure instead, which belongs under water line repair.

Shower valves and tub faucets

Shower valves hide behind tile, so the symptoms are what you can see: a handle that feels loose, water that turns scalding when someone flushes a toilet, or a tub spout that dribbles when the shower is on. Most single-handle valves have a replaceable cartridge, and a plumber can reach it from the trim side without opening the wall.

When the valve body itself is cracked or the stems are corroded, the wall has to be opened. We tell you that before starting, not after. Tub drains that clog or run slowly are a separate problem, and if the tub is the real trouble, drain cleaning is where that gets fixed.

Supply stops, supply lines and hidden leaks

The braided lines and angle stops under a sink are inexpensive parts that fail in expensive ways. A line that weeps slowly can rot a cabinet floor in months, and a stop that has not been turned in a decade often leaks the moment someone tries to close it. We replace the stop and the line together, because a new line on an old stop leaves the weakest part in place.

If you find a wet spot you cannot trace, do not assume it is the faucet. A leak behind a wall or under a slab can show up under a sink, and our leak detection work uses a pressure test and acoustic listening to find it before anything is cut open.

Hose bibs and outdoor spigots

Many lake-city homes have two or three outdoor spigots for boats, gardens and washdowns, and they take more abuse than any fixture indoors. A hose left connected holds pressure and cracks the valve, and the leak that follows runs inside the wall where nobody sees it. A hose bib that drips at the handle needs new packing, and one that weeps from the pipe behind it needs replacing.

A modest cold snap can also split an exposed spigot in Central Florida, so check each one after any night that drops near freezing. Tavares has reclaimed water for irrigation in some areas, so confirm a spigot is potable before you fill a pool or drink from it.

What faucet repair costs

A cartridge swap is a short visit. A new shower valve behind tile is another job entirely. These are the pieces that set the price.

Repair versus replacement.

A worn washer or cartridge is cheap. A faucet whose finish is pitted or whose parts are no longer made is better replaced.

Shutoff valve condition.

A stop that will not close adds a step, since the main valve has to be used and the stop replaced.

Access.

An open sink base is quick. A shower valve behind tile requires opening the wall.

Fixture quality.

Solid brass faucets from known brands cost more than lightweight ones and last longer.

Time of the call.

After-hours dispatch can add a cost, and you hear it before work starts.

A sudden valve failure that floods a kitchen is often covered for the water damage, but not for the faucet. A dripping faucet left for months is usually treated as maintenance, so photograph and report quickly.
