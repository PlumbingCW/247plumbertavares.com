Water Softener and Filtration in Tavares, FL

.

Water softener and filtration work in Tavares, FL means testing your water, then installing or repairing the equipment that fixes the problem you actually have. Softeners deal with minerals, filters handle taste, odor and sediment, and reverse osmosis cleans drinking water at one tap. They are three different tools, and buying the wrong one wastes money.

A licensed plumber can explain which one fits, install it, and repair the unit you already own. The price is explained before work starts, and same-day appointments are offered when available.

Start with a test, not a sales pitch

The City of Tavares Utilities water quality report does not list a hardness figure or a pH reading, so nothing printed tells you whether your tap water is hard. A home test kit costs little and gives a result within minutes. You can also ask City of Tavares Utilities what it knows about your area.

Do the test on a cold tap, after letting the water run a minute. Write the result down, and test again on a different day. Equipment sized to a wrong guess will either underperform or waste salt and water.

What each type of water equipment does.
EquipmentWhat it doesWhat it does not do
Water softener.Swaps hardness minerals for sodium or potassium to reduce scale and spotting.Does not improve taste or remove chlorine or bacteria.
Whole-house carbon filter.Reduces chlorine taste, odor and some chemicals at every tap.Does not soften water or remove dissolved minerals.
Sediment filter.Catches sand, rust flakes and silt before they reach fixtures.Does not touch taste or dissolved minerals.
Reverse osmosis at one tap.Cleans a drinking-water tap by pushing water through a fine membrane.Does not treat the rest of the house and wastes some water.

Product claims vary, so ask what a given model is rated to remove and what lab certification it carries.

Signs worth testing for

These symptoms can have several causes, so they are reasons to test, not proof.

  • White crust on faucets, showerheads and glass shower doors.
  • Spots on dishes or glassware after the dishwasher.
  • Soap that does not lather well, or a film on skin after a shower.
  • Low flow at a single showerhead caused by clogged holes.
  • A chlorine smell or taste at the tap.
  • Rust-colored stains in sinks and toilets.
  • A water heater that rumbles or takes longer to heat.
  • A rotten-egg smell from hot water only.

A rotten-egg smell from hot water alone usually points to a reaction in the water heater with its anode rod. A smell from both hot and cold points to the water supply instead, and the City of Umatilla's report notes it treats for hydrogen sulfide at its first plant.

What softeners and filters cannot do

A softener does not make water safe to drink if it was not, and it adds a small amount of sodium or potassium. It also needs salt or potassium refills, a drain line and power. A filter cartridge that is never changed becomes a place for bacteria to grow.

No unit is maintenance-free. If a salesperson says it is, or quotes a hardness reading for your tap without testing it, be careful. Ask what the unit will cost to run and what routine care it needs each year.

Tavares groundwater and chlorine

City of Tavares Utilities draws its water from the Floridan Aquifer through six wells ranging from 223 to 850 feet deep, with four water plant facilities. Water is disinfected with chlorine, and the 2023 average was 0.6 ppm. Chlorine taste is a common complaint and a carbon filter reduces it.

Because the report lists no hardness or pH, scale and spotting in your house are not something to blame on the city until a test says so. A private well has no city report at all, so a test is even more valuable there.

What water treatment costs

Water treatment ranges from a small cartridge filter to a whole-house system. These are the main price factors.

Type of equipment.

A single-tap RO unit, a whole-house filter and a softener differ in price and install work.

Size of the system.

Household size, number of bathrooms and flow needs decide the unit size.

Plumbing changes.

Bypass valves, a drain connection, a power outlet and a place to put the tank may be needed.

Running costs.

Salt or potassium, replacement cartridges and membranes are ongoing expenses.

Repair versus replacement.

A control valve repair costs less than a new unit, but an old tank may not be worth saving.

Insurance does not cover water treatment equipment, and scale damage is usually treated as wear. A leak from a failed tank is a different matter, and your policy may cover the water damage it causes.
