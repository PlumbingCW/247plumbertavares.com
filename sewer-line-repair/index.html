Sewage backing up right now: what to do first

A backup is both a mess and a health risk. These steps keep it from spreading until a plumber arrives.

Stop using water.

Do not flush, run the washer or use the dishwasher. Every gallon you add has nowhere to go but the lowest drain.

Keep people and pets out.

Raw sewage carries bacteria. Keep children and animals away and wear gloves and boots if you must enter.

Skip the chemicals.

Store-bought drain cleaners cannot fix a broken pipe, and they leave a hazard in the standing water that we then have to handle.

Take photos.

Photograph the backup and any damage for your insurer before cleanup starts.

If your neighbors also have backups, the blockage may be in the City main rather than your line. The City of Tavares lists an emergency sewer line on its Wastewater Division page, and calling it as well is a good idea.

Symptoms that point to a damaged sewer line

One slow drain is usually local. These signs, especially in combination, suggest the main line out of the house.

  • Gurgling from a toilet or tub when another fixture drains.
  • More than one fixture backing up at the same time.
  • Sewer smell in the yard or garage.
  • A wet or sunken patch in the lawn above the line.
  • A cleanout cap that is wet or has water standing in it.
  • Repeated clogs that come back a few weeks after cleaning.

What damages a sewer line

Roots are a common suspect. Fine roots enter through tiny joint gaps, grow into a mat, and trap paper and grease until the line clogs again and again. Each time the line is cleared the roots return, and each time they pry the joint a little wider.

Other causes are physical. Clay and old concrete pipe crack and shift, cast iron rusts through from the inside, and plastic pipe can sag where backfill settled, leaving a belly that holds water and waste. A heavy vehicle crossing a shallow line can finish off a weak pipe.

Cast iron and older homes

Cast iron drain pipe is common in much older homes, and it corrodes into a rough, scaly interior that catches debris long before it leaks. A camera shows the scale, and a cracked or flaking section can often be replaced in sections with modern pipe rather than redoing the whole run.

City sewer or septic: which one is yours?

Not every property near Tavares is on the City sewer. The City runs a wastewater system with 70 lift stations monitored 24/7/365 and a water reclamation facility on Woodlea Road, while some homes outside its service area use a septic tank. The repair is different for each, so ask the City which applies to your address.

On a city sewer connection, where your responsibility ends varies by property, so ask the City of Tavares which part of the lateral is yours. On septic, we repair the house plumbing and the line to the tank. Pumping a tank and rebuilding a drainfield generally belong to septic contractors, and we tell you plainly if your problem sits there.

How we find the break before we dig

A sewer camera on a flexible cable runs through the line and shows cracks, offsets, roots and bellies on a monitor. A locator at the surface marks the depth and path of the camera head, so the repair starts at an exact spot rather than a guess. If a line is full of waste, the camera cannot see through it, so it may need cleaning with a cable or a hydro jet first.

For a longer look at what the camera shows and where its limits are, see our sewer inspection page. For lines that are only clogged, hydro-jetting can clear grease and roots without any digging.

Trenchless or trench: how the options compare

Trenchless methods save lawns, driveways and tree roots, and they often finish faster. They are not a cure for every problem. A collapsed pipe, a large belly or a line that does not run continuously must be dug out.

What drives the cost of a sewer line repair

A sewer repair can be a small spot job or a long replacement, so the price is set after the camera work. Five factors matter most.

Depth and length of the damage.

A short section close to the surface is a smaller job than a long run under a driveway.

Repair method.

Lining, bursting and trench replacement differ in equipment, labor and surface restoration.

What is above the pipe.

Lawn is easy to restore. Concrete, pavers, trees and fences add time and cost.

Pipe material and condition.

Clay, cast iron and PVC need different fittings, and a badly corroded line may need more than a patch.

Cleaning before the camera.

A full line must be cleared before it can be inspected or lined.

Standard homeowners policies often exclude wear and tear and tree-root damage to sewer lines. Some carriers offer optional sewer or service-line coverage, so ask yours. Cleanup of a sudden backup inside the home is sometimes covered.

A phone call starts the process, and the plumber gives the price before touching a pipe.

When a backup comes from the line, not the drain

A backup that comes through the lowest drain in the house, such as a shower or floor drain, is usually a main line problem. Cleaning gets it moving again, and the camera shows why it clogged. If it clogs again soon after, the pipe itself is the cause and repair is the lasting answer.
